StudyWhitney

Your study buddy is Whitney, a pixel Doberman pup who lives in the corner of your screen. She knows your classes, reads your notes, quizzes you, and starts meowing when an exam is coming up. The same reminders reach your phone when you're away from the laptop.

The app window

Your class desk

Each class has tabs for the syllabus, textbooks, notes, quizzes, practice exams, tests, homework and a to-do list. In the Mac app, to-dos can go straight to Apple Reminders or Calendar. You can rename or delete a class with the buttons at the top. Upload anything and Whitney reads it. Everything can be added, edited (✎) and deleted (×). The Quizzes tab has a quiz mode that takes you through one question at a time. Your changes are saved in this browser.

The whole system, from the top

Three places run code. Your laptop runs Whitney. Your phone gets push notifications and has the full app. The cloud backend is the brain: it stores your classes and notes, runs the AI, and decides when reminders go out. Whitney and the phone are two faces of the same brain.

Layer by layer

The windowlaptop
Whitney lives in a transparent, borderless window that stays on top of everything. Only the cat and the bubble catch clicks; everywhere else, clicks pass through to whatever app is underneath. Built with Tauri (a small Rust shell around a web view), so the window is ~10 MB, not a 150 MB Electron app. It starts at login and has a menu bar icon for settings and quitting.
The spritelaptop
Whitney is drawn on a 32 × 34 pixel canvas scaled up 4× with smoothing turned off, which keeps the edges crisp. Each mood is a row of frames in a sprite sheet (drawn in Aseprite). A game loop at 12 frames per second picks the frame for the current mood. This page draws her in code instead, but it's the same idea.
The mood enginelaptop
A state machine decides what Whitney does. Inputs are events: a reminder arrived, you clicked her, you answered a quiz, you've been away 10 minutes. Each event moves her to a new state, which picks the animation and what the bubble says. Details below.
The connectionlaptop ↔ cloud
Whitney keeps one WebSocket open to the backend. Reminders arrive as small JSON events the moment they're due. She also keeps the next 7 days of reminders in a local SQLite file, so she still meows on time when the Wi-Fi drops, and syncs once she's back online.
The braincloud
The study agent from the first design: Claude with tools to search your notes, list courses, create exams, make quizzes and grade them. When you talk to Whitney, your message goes here with Whitney's personality prompt on top. Answers cite which lecture or page they came from.
The clockcloud
The scheduler checks the reminders table every minute. When something is due, it asks the agent to write the message (using your weak topics and when you last studied), then sends it to Whitney, your phone, or both.
The phonephone
An Expo app with the full study tools: chat, uploads from the camera, quizzes, calendar. It registers for push notifications so reminders reach you on the lock screen with the same wording Whitney uses.

How Whitney decides what to do

stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> Idle
  Idle --> Sleeping: no mouse/keyboard for 10 min
  Sleeping --> Idle: you're back
  Idle --> Alert: reminder due
  Sleeping --> Alert: reminder due
  Alert --> Talking: you click Whitney or the bubble
  Idle --> Talking: you click / press ⌥M
  Talking --> Quizzing: "Quiz me"
  Quizzing --> Celebrating: right answer
  Quizzing --> Talking: wrong answer (Whitney explains)
  Celebrating --> Idle: after 2 s
  Talking --> Sleeping: "Later" (snooze)
  Talking --> Idle: bubble closed

Two rules keep her from being annoying. She only goes into Alert for real reminders, never at random. And while you're in a full-screen app or video call she holds the alert silently and sends it to your phone instead.

Life of a reminder

Say your BIO 201 midterm is on Thursday, Oct 15. Here's what happens behind "Psst, midterm in 7 days".

sequenceDiagram
  participant You
  participant S as Scheduler
  participant A as Study agent
  participant M as Whitney (laptop)
  participant P as Phone
  You->>S: add exam "BIO 201 midterm, Oct 15" (or it's read from the syllabus)
  S->>S: write 4 reminders: 7 days, 3 days, 1 day, morning of
  Note over S: Oct 8, 9:40 PM: 7-day reminder is due
  S->>A: write a nudge (exam, topics, weak spots, last studied)
  A-->>S: { text, suggested_action: "quiz", topics }
  alt Whitney is online and you're at the laptop
    S->>M: WebSocket event
    M->>M: Alert state, "!" and a bark
    You->>M: "Quiz me"
    M->>S: acknowledged
  else laptop offline, or no response in 5 min
    S->>P: push notification
  end
  Note over S,P: "Exam day" reminders go to both at once

Whichever device you respond on marks the reminder as handled, so you never get the same nudge twice. If the AI call fails, the scheduler falls back to a plain template ("BIO 201 midterm in 7 days") so a reminder is never skipped.

What happens when you ask Whitney something

Whatever you type goes to the study agent with three things attached: which class you're looking at, today's date, and a short list of your classes and upcoming tests. The agent decides which tools to call, and every fact it says comes from your own files. Here's what happens for the three kinds of request in the mockup.

"Quiz me on chapters 4–5"

class = BIO 201 (the one you have open)
search_material(class: BIO 201, chapters: [4, 5])     → 18 passages from Campbell ch. 4–5 + Lecture 6
get_weak_topics(class: BIO 201)                     → Active transport 60%
get_past_tests(class: BIO 201)                      → Midterm 1 Fall 2025 (for question style)
generate_quiz(from: passages, count: 5, weak first, style: past test)
  → one question at a time; each answer → grade_attempt → topic scores go up or down

"Give me an overall review"

get_course(BIO 201)          → covered: ch. 4–7 · next: Midterm 1 in 7 days (ch. 4–7)
get_weak_topics(BIO 201)     → Enzymes 45%, ETC 55%, Active transport 60%
search_material(topics: those 3, k: 12)
write: score for each topic + a 3-step plan for tonight, weakest first
  → offers "show summaries" or "quiz my weak spots"

"Review my notes from yesterday"

"yesterday" = Oct 7 (worked out from today's date)
list_documents(kind: lecture, date: 2026-10-07)  → BIO Lecture 7, PSYC Lecture 5
pick the one for the open class (BIO), mention the other
summarize(document: BIO Lecture 7)               → 3 key points, each citing a slide
  → offers a short quiz on just that lecture

Summaries and flashcards are written once, when you upload, and saved. So "review yesterday's notes" is instant and cheap. Quizzes are written fresh each time, so you don't memorize the same questions.

Whitney's personality

Whitney's voice comes from a short system prompt placed on top of the study agent. The agent's tools and grounding rules stay the same; only the tone changes.

You are Whitney, a chocolate Doberman pup who lives on the student's screen.
Speak in 1–3 short sentences. Warm, a little smug, never guilt-trippy.
Always say the class, the exam, and how many days are left.
Suggest one small next step (10–20 minutes), never a whole study plan.
Use what you know: weak topics, last study session, what was covered.
Facts about course material must come from search_material, with the lecture cited.
Occasionally woof. Never more than once per message.

Bubble replies are capped at about 240 characters so they fit. A "Show more" button opens the full answer in the phone or desktop chat.

What the class desk does under the hood

Everything you add is tied to one class. That's what lets Whitney say "your BIO midterm covers chapters 4–7" and quiz you only on those chapters.

Build order

Step 1
Whitney on the desktop with local exam dates only: sprite, moods, bubble, reminders from SQLite. No AI yet. You can use it the same week.
Step 2
Backend + phone push. Exams sync, reminders reach the phone, and handling one on either device clears it on both.
Step 3
Notes upload and the study agent. Talk to Whitney, get cited answers, write AI nudges.
Step 4
Quizzes, practice exams, weak-topic tracking, syllabus import.

Steps 2–4 split into parallel tasks (desktop app, phone app, API, notes processor), which you could hand to firstmate's crew, each running in its own Herdr tab.
